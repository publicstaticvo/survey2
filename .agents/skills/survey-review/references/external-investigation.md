# External investigation: search and source access

Read this protocol before external searching, source retrieval or version checking. It implements the external branches of [contribution-investigation.md](contribution-investigation.md); use [state-and-cli.md](state-and-cli.md) for record fields. Internal manuscript-only checks stay in the contribution workflow. Return inspected evidence and bounded judgments to the same review loop.


**Input:** an existing K/X/A/D target or unnumbered discovery question in the run agenda, exact unsettled proposition, manuscript locations/citations, publication cutoff and version basis, and actual tools/access/budget. **Output:** actual S attempts; inspected, eligible positive and contrary E; an updated scoped assessment or unknown; a step naming the stopping reason. A tool/provider or API key is never mandatory.

This protocol governs evidence retrieval and source qualification. It does not replace the academic judgment steps in `contribution-investigation.md`; after retrieval, return to the relevant branch to decide correctness, increment, value, expectation or impact.

## 1. Decide whether external work is necessary

| Assessment/question | Internal evidence can settle | External work required before a substantive conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Correctness | Manuscript arithmetic from reported inputs, conflicting statements, definitions, logical sufficiency of an explicitly supplied argument | Fidelity to a cited study, actual experimental design/results, external counterexample, factual/causal claim not decidable from supplied premises |
| Relative increment | What the manuscript claims as its contribution; internal identity of the synthesis | Whether named prior accounts already supply it and what understanding is added at the cutoff; author self-positioning is insufficient |
| Value | Actual artifact supports a bounded use argument: what its table/distinction enables and under what conditions | Claims of field-wide importance, unresolved need, expert consensus, demonstrated effectiveness or knowledge the paper does not supply; an inferred potential use can stay explicitly reasoned rather than empirically demonstrated |
| Expected contribution | A concrete manuscript promise or necessary inferential prerequisite may establish a bounded obligation without external search | Field/reader-route discovery and independent topical importance; external issue still requires a separate argument that this survey owes it |

If the internal check establishes a logical support gap, do not pretend that it proves a source false or that no external evidence exists. Conversely, do not delay an obvious internal contradiction merely to perform a ceremonial search.

## 2. Form a discriminating search target

Write the proposition being tested and what observation would support, refute or leave it unresolved. Put this in S.query_target after executing the search, and in the target's current investigation plan before acting.

- **Correctness:** resolve the cited title/DOI/authors/identifier, then retrieve the specific definition, table, experiment or proof required. If the inference permits rival explanations, name the distinguishing variable/condition and search for its source evidence.
- **Increment:** express K as a concrete relation/classification/explanation/guidance, collect its alternative names from the manuscript and retrieved papers, and look for earlier accounts providing the same understanding. Start with cited or identified close works; expand through their references and relevant pre-cutoff neighboring accounts. Topic overlap only discovers candidates.
- **Value:** name the actual output, proposed user/problem and claimed improvement in understanding/judgment. Search for evidence of the problem, decision, conflict or use; compare whether K actually provides the needed distinctions/conditions. Do not substitute a novelty search for usefulness.
- **Field expectation:** discover real topic questions in independent accounts, then apply the separate scope/importance/feasibility gates. A credible source is evidence that an issue exists, not a rule that every survey must cover it.

Queries adapt after each result. Use exact titles/IDs for identity, terminology/relations for conceptual matches and citations for expansion; do not predefine one keyword template or require every strategy for every question.

## 3. Retrieve and qualify the source/version

1. Use available scholarly/general search, repository/publisher pages, cited originals or legitimate author copies. Prefer the original study for claims about its methods/results; use a survey for what it synthesizes. A reputable venue/name is not proof of the claim's correctness or of relevance.
2. Inspect whether the source actually addresses the **same proposition and conditions**. Exclude topical-but-nonmatching material from the decision while retaining useful discovery provenance.
3. Identify the exact substantive version and its availability date from version history/publication/preprint metadata. Confirm availability <= the run's unchanged cutoff. The access date and current search date do not establish historical availability. Do not use a recent revision's text under an earlier version's date; if only a date interval is known and it overlaps the cutoff, leave eligibility unresolved.
4. Read the decisive passage in the appropriate version. Search snippets/abstracts may establish identity or what the abstract says; they cannot settle an unobserved experiment/comparison. Store actual locator/excerpt and source identity in E. If the source date/version or passage cannot be checked, keep it partial/not_accessible rather than verified.
5. A post-cutoff result cannot confirm/refute historical novelty, impose an earlier missing contribution, or establish how the field stood then. Exclude its substantive evidence. It may be used only as a locator to an earlier source that must then be independently retrieved and checked; record this route and do not leak the later judgment into the verdict. If already exposed to such substantive findings, record the hindsight limitation.

## 4. Seek support and attempts to disconfirm

For a central scientific conclusion/challenge, inspect the direct evidence, the author's existing qualifications, and the strongest plausible counterexample/rival indicated by the evidence or reasoning. Make a concrete opposing search/inspection when such an alternative exists. If none was identified after examining the argument, state that scope rather than inventing an objection.

For increment, inspect the closest plausible pre-cutoff account even if it weakens novelty; compare actual synthesis passages, not just titles. For value, inspect whether the claimed use is supported by what the artifact supplies, and whether a missing condition makes it misleading. Positive and negative findings follow the same traceability discipline, but a modest potential-use claim does not require the same evidence as universal superiority or field consensus.

Record source passages as E without prejudging their interpretation. Assign support/counterevidence roles relative to the **current assessment**, state the proposition in purpose/reason, and preserve both sides when the verdict changes. Unavailable/contradictory sources are not quietly discarded. Explain source quality, applicability and comparability when evidence conflicts; do not vote by source count. Authoritative metadata verifies identity/date, not scientific truth.

## 5. Judge the bounded stopping condition

| Situation after actual work | May stop? / required record | Allowed conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Direct evidence and relevant author response inspected; grounded rival/counterevidence checked; no unresolved discriminator material to the stated claim | Yes: record tested proposition, decisive passages, opposing check and why further expansion is not currently needed | Scoped result, with remaining coverage limits; not universal correctness/absence |
| A qualifying prior account supplies the same specific synthesis | Yes for that identified overlap after actual passage comparison; continue only if needed to assess additional claimed increment | Prior account exists; named overlap and residual increment, not "all contributions already known" |
| Close accounts inspected and differences in a specific K established; planned discriminating comparisons addressed | Yes for relative comparison: name comparison set, actual differences and search bounds | Relative increment against inspected accounts, not first-ever/global novelty |
| A genuine problem/use and K's response established but broader benefit magnitude unmeasured | Yes for a bounded reasoned value assessment | Potential/argued value with conditions, not measured benefit or consensus |
| A supporting source found but a plausible decisive rival or contrary passage has not been checked | No if feasible within budget: investigate that discriminator next | Provisional result only; support alone is not sufficient stopping |
| No matching prior work returned | Revise terms/citation route where feasible; stop only after actual bounded search or access/budget constraint is stated | No prior match found in this search; global novelty remains unknown |
| Critical source/version unavailable, conflict remains, or budget ends before the needed opposing check | Stop this run with actual attempts, missing discriminator and relevance; park rather than retry endlessly | Unknown/limited, possibly overall uncertainty; no invented support gap or omission |
| A clear feasible discriminating test was never attempted and no actual constraint prevents it | No: keep pending | No terminal unknown merely by filling a field |

Search "sufficiency" is the reviewer's reasoned stopping judgment about this proposition, not a fixed source quota or mechanical saturation test. A failed attempt may end an iteration but not settle the question. If access conditions permit another materially different useful route, try it; repeated denied requests without changed conditions are not productive evidence gathering.

## 6. Bound the conclusion and update

| Claim sought | Minimum relevant evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| A prior work exists | Identity, qualifying date/version, inspected matching contribution passage | Establishes that precedent only, not complete duplication or a universal obligation to cite it |
| K has relative increment | K passage plus actual close-account comparison showing added/clarified understanding | Relative to compared accounts; no inference from search failure to global novelty |
| K is correct at scope | Required source facts/conditions/inference and pertinent opposing evidence inspected | The audited inference only; unknown components retained |
| K has scholarly value | Actual artifact -> identifiable problem/use -> reason it helps -> conditions, with external support where the importance claim needs it | Novelty alone does not imply value; distinguish reasoned potential, measured use and consensus |
| Survey owes an expected synthesis | Real issue + survey-specific obligation + material importance + feasible pre-cutoff response, then adequate manuscript presence search | Source credibility alone and reader preference do not establish duty |

Write S/E, update the relevant A/K/D/X and any affected I, record the actual search stopping reason and unknowns, then step. Re-sort the active agenda. Maintain the original cutoff and evidence links through final judgment; an indirect reference cannot make an excluded later source admissible.

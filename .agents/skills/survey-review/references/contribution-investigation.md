# Contribution investigation: action branches

Use this file during the log-driven loop. Each branch specifies the input, actions, state changes and completion condition. Perform the relevant branch for the chosen question; do not execute every branch as a form for every paragraph. Record actual source passages in E and actual external attempts in S according to [state-and-cli.md](state-and-cli.md).

## 1. Identify an existing contribution

| Field | Operating rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | A passage, table, figure or distributed argument appears to provide a synthesis. |
| Input | Manuscript purpose, candidate K, and located manuscript locations. |
| Actions | Read the output; define its unit; separate author text, source findings and reviewer reconstruction; set scope and centrality. |
| Output | Create or update K, its locations, identity, dependencies and three unchecked assessment dimensions. |
| Done when | The supplied understanding has a clear scope and granularity, or the candidate is returned to the agenda. |
| Do not conclude | A title, contribution list or reviewer reconstruction is not proof that the paper supplies the contribution. |

**Input:** manuscript goal, candidate K, and the passages/figures/tables said to supply it.

1. Read the actual output, not just the author's contribution list. Write what understanding it provides: which entities it connects, what distinctions/conditions it establishes, or what evidence/guidance it organizes.
2. Identify its unit. A descriptive inventory may support navigation when it gives coherent organization; a list of citations or repeated source findings alone does not establish a cross-study conclusion. Avoid assuming only novel theories count.
3. Locate support across sections if needed. Distinguish the survey's conclusion, its cited sources' conclusions, and your own reconstruction. If you can supply a missing connection only by doing new synthesis yourself, record that gap; do not credit your reconstruction as an existing K.
4. Check granularity. Combine descriptions of the same output; split outputs only when their supporting arguments or scholarly uses differ materially. Record actual K dependencies, not topic similarity.
5. Update statement, locations and identity/centrality reason. Keep uninvestigated assessments unchecked.

**Done:** K states a located output at a clear scope and granularity. If no such output can be located, retain the question in the run agenda and revise the candidate; do not declare "no synthesis" from the abstract alone.

## 2. Discover reasonable expected contributions

| Field | Operating rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | A manuscript promise, inference prerequisite or independent field issue suggests a synthesis the survey might owe. |
| Input | Topic, survey scope, cutoff, current K records and actual discovery leads. |
| Actions | Form a concrete question; investigate the manuscript or field route; record E/S; apply all four screening gates. |
| Output | Create or update D; create exploratory X only when the candidate passes screening. |
| Done when | Each investigated route has a reasoned disposition; unperformed screening remains an agenda item. |
| Do not conclude | Do not turn every reader preference, search failure or rhetorical promise into a missing contribution. |

**Input:** topic, survey type/scope, author promises, and the current coverage/discovery record. This branch discovers candidates; it does not begin with a mandatory omission.

### Manuscript route

| Field | Operating rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | The manuscript promises an output or depends on an unstated synthesis. |
| Input | Goals, principal outputs, promise passages and relevant K/A context. |
| Actions | Extract the promise; formulate the needed understanding; record its E basis; screen it. |
| Output | A D with an actual disposition, followed by exploratory X only if qualified. |
| Done when | The promise has been screened or the unresolved screening task remains explicitly pending. |
| Do not conclude | A vague ambition is not a required contribution without scope, use and feasibility grounds. |

1. Read the survey's stated goals and principal outputs. Identify what must be understood for them to deliver their stated purpose.
2. Form a concrete question and explain the dependency. For example, a claimed cross-study efficiency ranking requires compatible cost definitions; a taxonomy used only for navigation does not automatically require mutually exclusive categories.
3. Extract the promise and relevant comparison/inference passages into E. Keep the question and proposed feasible synthesis in the run agenda while screening is still unperformed.
4. Apply the screening below, then create/update D with the actual outcome and grounds. The required D disposition is a screening outcome, not a placeholder for a plan. A promise can be vague or unreasonable; do not mechanically convert every rhetorical ambition into a mandatory output.

### Independent field/reader route

| Field | Operating rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | The topic/cutoff suggests an important relationship, dispute or evidence limit not exposed by manuscript promises. |
| Input | Actual topic, justified scope, cutoff and an unresolved field question. |
| Actions | Search adaptively; inspect substantive passages; distinguish source claims from reviewer importance; screen before creating X. |
| Output | S/E provenance and a D disposition, or a documented limited route. |
| Done when | The route yields a qualified candidate, a reasoned rejection, or an auditable access/coverage limit. |
| Do not conclude | A snippet, citation frequency or one source automatically establishes an obligation for this survey. |

1. Starting from the survey's actual topic and cutoff, investigate independent scholarly accounts of its important relationships, disputes, choices or evidence limits. Choose queries from the unresolved question, then adapt after reading. Follow relevant citations and neighboring surveys where useful.
2. Read substantive passages that explain the issue. A source title, search snippet, citation frequency or model recollection is a discovery lead, not a complete obligation argument. Check cutoff versions before using a source as historical grounds.
3. Form a compact external problem sketch: the question, the actual sources exposing it, why it matters, and a synthesis achievable from existing literature. Keep the source's view distinct from your inference about importance. Until screening is attempted, keep the sketch in the run agenda rather than inventing a D disposition.
4. Apply screening, then record the sketch and actual outcome in D, **before** claiming the manuscript lacks the expected response. If access fails, record limited discovery with attempts and missing evidence. Do not manufacture a field sketch to satisfy the route requirement.

No fixed keyword template, provider or paper quota is required. One strong source may establish a real issue, but neither one nor many sources automatically prove a duty for this particular survey. Source disagreement can itself be evidence worth synthesizing; do not silently turn a contested view into consensus.

### Screening: four questions

| Field | Operating rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | A concrete discovery lead has been formulated as a possible expected synthesis. |
| Input | Basis E/S, survey scope, possible use and feasible literature response. |
| Actions | Test reality, obligation, material importance and feasibility in order; narrow the expectation when only part passes. |
| Output | D disposition; exploratory X only for a candidate. |
| Done when | All four gates have evidence or the failing/unknown gate and reopening condition are recorded. |
| Do not conclude | `insufficient` is not a placeholder for work not performed; `limited` is not evidence that the expectation is absent. |

For each candidate, answer the four questions in D.reason/scope_and_use/possible_synthesis, citing basis E and S:

| Question | Pass evidence | If it does not pass |
|---|---|---|
| Is the issue real? | Located manuscript promise, logical prerequisite or external passages establish the question | `insufficient`; state the missing support |
| Does this survey reasonably owe a response? | Its justified topic/type/purpose makes the understanding part of its task; explain why existing coverage cannot serve that task without it | `optional` or `out_of_scope`; reader preference alone does not pass |
| Is it important for the claimed contribution or a justified scholarly use? | A specific comparison, explanation, interpretation or research choice becomes materially less reliable/usable | `optional` if only a nice addition; `insufficient` if effect is unknown |
| Could a survey feasibly supply it at the cutoff? | Existing literature permits a synthesis, or a justified account of uncertainty/conflicting evidence | `out_of_scope`/`optional` if it demands new experiments or a new research project; `limited` if investigation cannot decide |

When all pass, mark D `candidate` and create exploratory X. State an achievable **kind of response**, allowing equivalent adequate syntheses; do not prescribe your preferred taxonomy or a unique phrasing.

All four questions are necessary gates for the **specific expectation**, not demands for certainty about every detail. Unknown factual grounds, scope/obligation, material importance or feasibility blocks promotion of that omission. A known important scoped obligation need not wait for an incidental detail such as the exact size of reader benefit or which of several adequate presentations is best. Narrow the expectation to what passes and retain peripheral uncertainty. If a necessary gate fails, close the rejected D with reasons; if investigated but unresolved, close limited/insufficient with actual work and reopening conditions. Unperformed screening remains pending in the run agenda, not a completed disposition.

When work on an actual contribution exposes a different missing synthesis, still use this screening. When a contribution's existing inference is flawed, use A/K; do not inflate every flaw into a new expected contribution.

**Done:** the route is evidenced and every investigated candidate has a disposition. Having no omission is an acceptable result. These screening rules operationalize a reasoned judgment; they do not provide an objective oracle for all reader expectations.

## 3. Check X rationality and presence separately

| Field | Operating rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | A screened candidate D has produced exploratory X. |
| Input | X grounds, D screening result, cutoff and a whole-paper search plan. |
| Actions | Recheck obligation; search for equivalent responses; inspect all relevant sections; classify presence; record counterevidence; assess effect. |
| Output | Update X rationality/presence/missing states and create or update I only for an established effect. |
| Done when | Necessity and presence each have a justified result and the whole-paper check is recorded. |
| Do not conclude | An absent phrase is not an absent synthesis; X alone is never an automatic penalty. |

**Input:** exploratory X and candidate D with grounds.

1. Recheck the obligation argument independently of whether the desired text appears. Could a different reasonable survey of this declared scope fulfill its purpose without that synthesis? If yes, explain whether optional or whether the actual manuscript made a stronger promise.
2. Search for equivalent answers, not only expected keywords. Inspect relevant sections, definitions, tables/figures, discussion, limitations and appendices. Record locations and actual content in E. Inspect the original PDF if extraction omits visual evidence.
3. Classify presence: present, partial, absent, or unknown. If partial, name what is supplied and the exact remaining gap. A limitation may honestly state uncertainty, which can be an adequate response; it need not solve an open research problem.
4. Consider counterevidence: a distributed answer, an explicit scope boundary, a conditional claim or an existing reference may change the expectation or its assessed presence. Citation alone is not necessarily synthesis, but do inspect its role.
5. If obligation, materiality and an unmet component are established, set X decision-relevant with confirmed missing status; record I explaining the lost understanding/use. Otherwise keep exploratory, mark satisfied/optional/unsupported/unknown as appropriate and update D.

**Done:** necessity and presence each have a justified result, whole-paper search coverage is recorded, and any negative effect has its own argument. The outcome may be that the expectation was the reviewer's preference and should not influence quality.

## 4. Audit correctness and scientific reasoning

| Step | What to do | Do not infer directly |
|---|---|---|
| Reconstruct | Rebuild source facts, comparison conditions, inference and survey conclusion. | The reviewer's reconstruction is not automatically the author's argument. |
| Verify | Check decisive sources, definitions, quantities and conditions. | A title, abstract or citation cannot replace decisive evidence. |
| Test | Examine viable counterexamples, competing explanations and the author's qualifications. | Do not manufacture opposition merely to create a weakness. |
| Adjudicate | Update A and propagate the bounded result to affected K/X. | `qualified` does not automatically mean a defect. |
| Assess impact | Decide what changes for overall understanding and scholarly use. | A local error is not automatically an overall quality decline. |

| Field | Operating rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | A consequential K/X inference needs confirmation or challenge. |
| Input | Its stated scope, premises, comparison conditions and possible alternative. |
| Actions | Follow the five steps above; record supporting and contrary E; update A; propagate only established effects. |
| Output | A bounded A verdict and, where justified, K/X and I updates. |
| Done when | The inference is supported, qualified, invalid, misrepresented, a reasonable hypothesis or explicitly unresolved. |
| Do not conclude | A rejected challenge is not a weakness, and an unresolved audit is not a negative factual finding. |

**Input:** a consequential inference in K/X, its expressed scope and its source/comparison premises. Use A even for confirmation; a manufactured objection is not required.

1. **Reconstruct the unit:** source facts -> comparison/selection conditions -> inference -> survey synthesis. Mark explicit links, fair implicit reconstruction and missing support separately. For a taxonomy/map, inspect its definitions, placements and actual intended use rather than inventing a causal theorem.
2. **Check source fidelity:** read the decisive original study/version; distinguish its results from the survey's paraphrase. Verify definitions, quantities, experimental roles and conclusions at the passage needed. If access fails, mark that component unknown.
3. **Check comparability and logic:** identify populations, datasets, metrics, baselines, compute accounting, intervention and scope where relevant. Ask whether the premises license the actual conclusion. Internal inconsistency can be checked from the manuscript without external search.
4. **Form a grounded challenge where warranted:** state a rival explanation, counterexample or condition and the fact/logical reason making it plausible. "Why not B?" without a reason B matters stays a question, not a defect. Seek evidence that could reject your preferred challenge as well as support it.
5. **Check the author's response:** inspect definitions, discussion, limitations, appendices and every stronger formulation in abstract/conclusion/summary tables. Note whether caveats are both present and adequate for the claims they qualify.
6. **Adjudicate:** update A with actual investigation, positive and contrary E, a verdict and its scope. Distinguish the four challenge outcomes in state-and-cli.md: sustained, answered by an adequate author qualification, investigated but unresolved, and refuted by counterevidence. The answered and refuted outcomes both use not_warranted, but retain their distinct reasons; a refutation requires explicit withdrawal and downstream updates. Use qualified for the evidence boundary and separately determine whether the manuscript crosses it. A useful, honestly labeled hypothesis need not be a validated causal result.
7. **Propagate:** update K correctness and relevant dependent K/X. Create/update I only for the effect established. Do not carry a rejected challenge into overall reasoning through a different wording.

**Done:** the specific inference is supported, qualified, invalid, misrepresented, a reasonable hypothesis, or explicitly unresolved; the author's response and strongest relevant counterevidence have been considered. This is a bounded audit, not proof of universal correctness.

## 5. Assess relative increment

| Field | Operating rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | K identity is established and a relative comparison is needed. |
| Input | K output, named pre-cutoff accounts and their substantive passages. |
| Actions | Retrieve relevant prior accounts; compare distinctions, relations, evidence boundaries and guidance; record limits. |
| Output | Update K baseline and increment status/reason. |
| Done when | A source-grounded comparison exists, or the missing evidence is explicitly recorded as unknown. |
| Do not conclude | Failure to find prior work is not proof of novelty; topical similarity is not proof of no utility. |

**Input:** the located K output and pre-cutoff accounts actually retrieved.

1. Ask what understanding already existed, not merely whether the taxonomy's name or figure is new. Find a relevant prior survey or account and inspect its substantive synthesis.
2. Compare the specific output: distinctions, relations, evidence boundaries, explanations, organization or guidance. Record overlap and what is added, clarified, connected, corrected or made more useful.
3. Distinguish genuine reorganization/clarification from cosmetic relabeling. Useful integration can add understanding without introducing a new scientific finding.
4. Update baseline_or_prior and increment_reason with precise source locations and search limits. Choose the qualitative state relative to **inspected** prior accounts, not the entire field.
5. If only titles/abstracts or inaccessible sources were found, keep the substantive comparison unknown. An author comparison table is evidence of the author's positioning, not independent confirmation of novelty.

**Done:** a source-grounded relative comparison or an explicit unknown with missing evidence. Failure to find prior work is not proof of novelty; similarity does not automatically eliminate utility.

## 6. Assess scholarly value

| Field | Operating rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | K correctness boundaries and actual output are sufficiently understood. |
| Input | What K supplies, its conditions, scope and justified scholarly use. |
| Actions | Name the output; identify a concrete use; test whether the artifact supports that use; weigh correctness, increment and counterevidence. |
| Output | Update K significance and create/update I for material established value. |
| Done when | Output -> use -> reason -> conditions is explicit, or the value judgment remains bounded unknown. |
| Do not conclude | Source count, contribution count, author confidence or novelty alone proves value. |

**Input:** what K actually supplies, its correctness boundaries, and the survey's justified scope/use.

1. Name a specific output: for example a distinction between evidence of task performance and evidence of an internal mechanism, rather than "a comprehensive overview."
2. State the scholarly use it supports: understanding a relationship, interpreting conflicting findings, choosing methods under constraints, locating reliable evidence, or identifying a justified research question.
3. Explain why this particular output helps that use. Inspect whether the text/table actually contains the relations, conditions or reasoning required. If guidance is only generic, say which needed distinction it does not provide.
4. Check correctness/increment dependencies. A useful navigation map may survive a flawed causal explanation; an invalid ranking may not guide method choice. Similarity to prior work limits increment but does not alone show no value.
5. Distinguish potential utility argued from the artifact from demonstrated reader benefit or expert consensus. Use independent field evidence for consequential relevance where available; record disagreements/limits. Do not demand a user study for every value judgment.
6. Update significance_reason/status and I for material established value. Apply the same specificity and counterevidence standards used for weaknesses.

**Done:** output -> use -> reason -> conditions is explicit, with a reasoned assessment or a stated unknown. Do not infer value from contribution count, source count, author confidence or reviewer enthusiasm.

## 7. Connect findings to the overall judgment

| Field | Operating rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | K/X/A findings have been individually assessed and may affect the final evaluation. |
| Input | Eligible evidence, established and contrary findings, remaining uncertainty, fixed task and scale. |
| Actions | Separate finding from impact; deduplicate causes; test counterevidence; weigh positive and negative material evidence; relate the balance to the fixed task. |
| Output | Nonduplicated I records, updated review points and one prose overall rationale. |
| Done when | The rationale explains what changes for the survey's use and why the chosen category or qualitative judgment follows. |
| Do not conclude | Do not average labels, count findings, or convert an unresolved/access issue directly into a quality penalty. |

**Input:** assessed K/X/A, their eligible supporting and contrary evidence, outstanding uncertainty, and the evaluation task and scale fixed at initialization. **Output:** nonduplicated I records and an overall argument using that same task/scale. This is a reasoning procedure, not a score aggregation algorithm.

1. **Settle the finding separately.** State exactly what the evidence establishes and at which scope, using K/X/A. A wrong number, an unsupported inference and an inaccessible source are different findings. Preserve the factual outcome even if its overall effect is negligible. A supported contribution is likewise not automatically a major strength.
2. **Explain materiality and severity.** Identify the affected synthesis, conclusion, evidence base or scholarly use, then describe the consequence: a local correction preserving the conclusion, a narrower usable claim, loss of a major inference, or a broader reliability limitation. Reconstruct evidence -> premise/inference changed -> understanding/use affected in I.reason; state what survives. `extent=main` locates the effect; it does not mean severe. No new severity enum or numeric weight is required.
3. **Group common causes and dependencies.** Use I.overlaps to identify repeated manifestations of the same root or dependent consequence. Explain the combined impact once in the overall argument; multiple affected conclusions can enlarge its scope without counting the same error repeatedly. Conversely, several small independent errors can form an evidenced pattern, but neither their count nor a single anecdote establishes global unreliability.
4. **Test the effect against contrary evidence.** Inspect author qualifications, alternative support and unaffected outputs. A map may retain navigation value after one causal explanation fails. If a premise or the materiality link is unsettled, leave I uncertain and report the bounded uncertainty. Rejected challenges must be withdrawn from I and final points; do not reintroduce them under a broader label.
5. **Form the qualitative balance.** Explain the strongest reliably useful outputs, the decisive limitations and why they constrain those outputs, and the remaining value. Include positive and negative material evidence without equal-count quotas or a requirement that every finding move the rating. If the finding has no demonstrated material effect, keep it as a bounded comment or working observation rather than inflating it into a decisive weakness.
6. **Relate that balance to the fixed scale, if requested.** Following ProReviewer's ordinary reviewer judgment, explain why the established balance warrants the chosen category and why the nearest plausible alternative is less appropriate. This is not an arithmetic sum of dimensions, K labels, severity words or I counts. Ask whether the unresolved question could plausibly change that category; explain why or why not. A resolved finding need not change the category. Do not switch from academic quality to acceptance prediction or another venue's standard at output time.
7. **Resolve the remaining action.** Use the table below, update the relevant records and judgment, and re-sort remaining work. If no score can be defended, explain the decisive unknown and retain the findings that can be assessed; do not invent a score or turn an access failure into a paper defect. A requested qualitative evaluation needs no numeric score merely to satisfy a template.

| Condition | Action | Completion / allowed outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Finding established; contribution/use effect untested | Investigate the specific dependency and surviving support | I established if the link is supported; otherwise uncertain or no decisive I |
| Central location but minor bounded correction | Record the narrow consequence and preserved output | May merit a comment or limited weakness without changing the overall category |
| Same root supports several negative or positive descriptions | Link overlaps/dependencies and explain their joint scope | One combined material argument, not repeated penalties/bonuses |
| Challenge answered or refuted | Update A/K/X, withdraw dependent negative I and remove obsolete outline points | Current judgment uses neither withdrawn criticism nor its stale score rationale |
| Unknown actually investigated; unlikely to change category for stated reasons | Retain limits alongside established balance | A supported category may be given; unknown is neither proof of neutrality nor an automatic veto |
| Unknown could change category; feasible discriminating action remains | Perform that action before finalizing the judgment | Reassess finding, impact and category; do not close merely because unknown was entered |
| Decisive unknown remains after actual bounded investigation | Explain its plausible effect and why the evidence cannot discriminate | Qualitative assessment or inability to rate under the fixed task, not an adverse factual finding |

**Done:** finding, materiality/severity and category impact are distinguishable; the overall rationale jointly weighs nonduplicated positive and negative evidence under the original task/scale and states consequential uncertainty. This remains an argued reviewer judgment, not a structurally certified quality score or demonstrated expert consensus. It does not claim exhaustive coverage of every academic quality dimension.

### General quality issues encountered during contribution work

When a numerical, source, consistency or writing issue is encountered, first ask what actual role it plays in a K/X premise, synthesis or use. Apply the same factual audit and materiality procedure; no extra general-quality search pass is introduced. A definition error can distort a taxonomy, an incompatible denominator can defeat a cross-study comparison, and an ambiguous condition can make guidance unusable. A typo that leaves the interpretation intact need not affect the overall judgment.

Do not force every issue into a newly invented K. If a serious, evidenced issue affects trust or usability independently of an identifiable K/X, retain the concrete source passages in eligible E and use I.independent_quality with an explicit factual verification and direct impact argument only when established. An unattached untested suspicion stays in the run agenda, not an independent-quality penalty. A repeated pattern requires distinct inspected examples and an argument about their joint consequence; do not extrapolate from one mistake to fabricated literature or global unreliability.

Record material unlinked issues and whether their impact could be expressed without forcing a contribution link. They reveal a possible boundary of the contribution-centered hypothesis. This logging has no recall guarantee: the incidence and importance of issues not encountered by this workflow remain unknown, and cannot be estimated from four development runs or from its own detected cases alone.

## External investigation

Before external searching or source retrieval, read [external-investigation.md](external-investigation.md). It defines the trigger table, question-specific search targets, source/version and cutoff checks, support and counterevidence handling, stopping conditions and allowed conclusions.
